Puppy packet insert: "Growing up safe at home"

5.5 × 8.5 in, portrait

A checklist that earns its place in the puppy packet on its own, with containment as one item among several. It gets kept because it's useful, and it names your Pro because they supplied it.

A checklist for new dog owners

Growing up safe at home

  • ID and microchip. A collar tag with your phone number, and a microchip registered to you. Update both if you move.
  • Vaccines and vet visits. Keep the schedule your veterinarian gives you, and ask about parasite prevention for your area.
  • The yard. Walk it before your dog does. Look for gaps under gates, toxic plants, unsecured pools and open sheds.
  • Containment. A fenced yard, a leash, or an invisible fence. Tie-outs and "he just stays close" are how dogs end up in the road. If a physical fence isn't practical for your property, an Out of Sight Fencing system installed and trained by a local professional keeps your dog home with nothing to build.
  • Training and socializing. Short, positive sessions, early and often. A puppy class pays for itself.
  • Household hazards. Chocolate, grapes, xylitol, medications, cords and small toys. Keep them out of reach.
  • Car rides. A harness or crate, every time.
  • A plan for "he got out." Recent photo on your phone, a neighbor with your number, and your local shelter's number saved.

Keeping partners warm
A kit in a drawer sends no referrals. The cadence is the program.

The 90-day cadence

Day 0Drop-off visit. Log the partner, the contact, and the code.
Day 14Text the front desk: "Need more cards?" Restock if yes. This visit is the one most Pros skip, and it's the one that matters.
Day 30Stop by with the one thing you promised (cards, a coffee) and one story: an install nearby, a customer video.
Day 60Share a result. "Your card sent us the Millers on Oak St. Thank you." Even if it was only a call.
Day 90Coffee or lunch with your top 3 partners. Ask what their customers ask. Repeat the cycle.

When a referral converts

  • Within 48 hours: text the partner. Name the customer if the customer says it's fine, otherwise the street.
  • Within a week: deliver the thank-you they chose: .
  • Ask the customer for a video on your testimonial link. A partner's referral on video is the best restock gift you'll ever bring.
  • Realtor referrals: send the agent a photo of the finished yard with the dog in it. Agents share those.

Partner thank-you, their choice

Offer every partner the same menu and let them pick: a gift card, a donation in their name to a local rescue, or credit toward a Dog Guard® system of their own. Vets usually take the donation, agents usually take the gift card, and trainers often want the system. Set the amount once and keep it.

Record it or it didn't happen

  • Every partner gets a code. Put it on their referral cards and in the QR.
  • When a lead mentions a code or scans a partner QR, enter the code in the lead source field in Zoho.
  • Log each partner visit as a note on the partner record. The home office scorecard counts partner activity as effort, alongside reviews and testimonials.
  • Once a quarter, look at the tracker: partners with zero referrals after 90 days get one more visit, then get replaced.

What good looks like

Ten active partners, four restock visits a month, and three referral installs a quarter in year one. Most Pros find two partners carry the program. Find those two and take care of them.

Program notes for review

What this draft assumes, and what is still open. Nothing here prints.

Decisions built into the draft

  • Dealers print from files. Every offer is therefore a dealer-level offer, and the credit amount and partner thank-you are fill-in fields, not home office commitments.
  • The realtor certificate is a free consultation plus a fill-in credit.
  • Partner thank-you is "partner's choice" (gift card, rescue donation, or credit toward their own system) so the program doesn't depend on the old vet program's assumption that every partner wants a fence of their own.
  • Every partner (realtor, community, builder, practice or shop) gets a code. The QR on partner-specific pieces points at the dealer hub link with ?ref=CODE appended, so scans can be attributed once the hub page records the parameter.
  • Product claims are kept general (brief, safe correction; fraction of the cost of a physical fence; 35+ years American-made). No dollar figures for physical fencing, no training timelines.

Still open

  • The Developer insert assumes three deal structures (upgrade option, move-in amenity, model-home referral) and leads with the dog objection. Adjust once the dealers who started builder relationships report how their deals were actually structured.
  • The Veterinarian insert deliberately offers no cash or fence credit to the practice; the thank-you is a rescue donation in the practice's name. Confirm this is the direction you want before it goes to dealers.
  • The HOA insert uses a "community code" per community so referrals from a welcome packet can be tracked like any partner.
  • Whether the hub page can read the ref parameter and pass it into Zoho as the lead source. If not, the code on the card still works by phone.
  • A brand-review pass on the safety language once you're happy with the copy.
  • Print-ready PDF and Word versions of each piece, produced after the copy is approved.
